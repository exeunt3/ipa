## Overview

The standard rock ensemble (drums, bass, guitar, voice) functions as an intensive protocol for distributed attention and collective timing. Unlike composed music, its core operation lies in the real-time negotiation of groove, volume, restraint, and escalation.

## Roles

- Drums: temporal anchoring and pressure modulation
- Bass: harmonic gravity and groove continuity
- Guitar: textural variance and punctuated articulation
- Voice (optional): semantic and affective overlay

## Procedure

1. Establish a shared tempo through repetition rather than counting.
2. Lock bass and drums before introducing harmonic complexity.
3. Allow parts to simplify under pressure rather than escalate.
4. Use breakdowns as resets of collective attention.
5. End sessions deliberately; fatigue degrades coherence.

## Notes on Intensity

Intensity emerges from interdependence. The protocol peaks when individual agency is partially surrendered to the group dynamic without disappearing.

## Expected Effects

- Time compression
- Loss of self-conscious monitoring
- Heightened sensitivity to micro-errors
- Collective flow states
