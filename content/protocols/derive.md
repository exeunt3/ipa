## Overview

The dérive (French for "drift") is a Situationist protocol for psychogeographic exploration. Participants move through urban terrain according to affective currents rather than functional destinations. The aim is to perceive the city as a field of ambient forces—zones of attraction, repulsion, transition, and stagnation—and to document the emotional topology that commercial navigation obscures.

## Materials

- Comfortable walking shoes
- Optional: notebook, camera, or voice recorder for documentation
- Optional: dice, cards, or other chance operation tools
- No maps or GPS (or commitment to ignore them)

## Procedure

1. Begin at a location with multiple exit routes (intersection, plaza, transit hub).
2. Choose initial direction by chance operation or gut attraction.
3. Walk without destination, attending to shifts in atmosphere and affect.
4. When reaching a decision point, pause and sense which path "pulls."
5. Document observations: architectural moods, liminal zones, temporal textures.
6. Allow encounters with strangers, obstacles, or surprises to redirect the drift.
7. Set a time limit or geographic boundary to contain the practice.
8. Conclude with a debrief: map the emotional terrain traversed.

## Variants

- **Algorithmic dérive**: Use a rule set (e.g., "turn left at every red door").
- **Collaborative dérive**: Group splits and reconvenes to compare affective maps.
- **Night dérive**: Conduct after dark to access different urban rhythms.
- **Constraint dérive**: Navigate only through zones of a single type (alleys, parks, industrial edges).

## Notes on Intensity

Intensity arises from sustained attention and surrender of control. The protocol succeeds when the city feels less like a container and more like a living organism with moods and intentions.

## Expected Effects

- Defamiliarization of known environments
- Heightened perception of ambient affect
- Time distortion (hours feel like minutes)
- Discovery of hidden or overlooked spaces
- Sense of the city as a conscious or semi-conscious field
