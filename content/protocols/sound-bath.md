## Overview

A sound bath is a protocol of sustained harmonic immersion using continuous or slowly modulated sound sources (bowls, gongs, drones, voice, electronics). The aim is not performance, healing, or trance induction, but the cultivation of a shared acoustic field that reorganizes attention, bodily orientation, and temporal perception.

## Materials

- Sustained sound sources (e.g. singing bowls, gongs, drones, feedback-capable instruments)
- A space with minimal visual distraction
- Participants seated or supine

## Procedure

1. Establish silence and stillness in the room.
2. Introduce sound gradually, privileging continuity over rhythm.
3. Allow overlaps and interference patterns to develop naturally.
4. Avoid narrative cues or verbal framing during the session.
5. Conclude by tapering amplitude rather than stopping abruptly.

## Notes on Intensity

Intensity arises from duration and resonance, not loudness. The protocol succeeds when sound ceases to feel external and instead appears as a spatial condition the body is inside of.

## Expected Effects

- Altered sense of bodily boundary
- Temporal dilation
- Heightened sensitivity to micro-variation
- Non-symbolic affective states
